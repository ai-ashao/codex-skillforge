# Scoring Rubric

Score each criterion from 0 to 5. Weighted points are:

`weighted_points = raw_score / 5 × weight`

Round displayed weighted points to one decimal place. Keep full precision for totals.

## Opportunity score: 100 points

### A. Search opportunity — 39 points

#### 1. Independent primary keyword — weight 12

Question: Is there a clear, stable, non-invented keyword cluster that naturally represents an independent product or homepage?

- 0: No discoverable keyword or only an internally invented concept.
- 1: Ambiguous demand, mixed intent, or almost entirely brand-navigation demand.
- 2: Some relevant queries, but low coherence, weak stability, or only one narrow phrase.
- 3: Clear primary term plus several close variants supporting one focused page.
- 4: Coherent cluster with stable intent and enough breadth for an independent homepage.
- 5: Strong, stable, validated cluster with multiple independent competitors or clear first-party query evidence.

#### 2. SERP breakability — weight 15

Question: Can a new entrant plausibly enter the SERP through product fit, page quality, specialization, or a weak result set?

- 0: Dominated by highly relevant, excellent, authoritative products with no credible wedge.
- 1: Strong specialist incumbents; candidate has no meaningful advantage.
- 2: Some weak results, but authority/link gap or product parity remains severe.
- 3: Mixed SERP; several beatable results and a plausible differentiation path.
- 4: Many weak, outdated, indirect, thin, or poorly localized results; clear execution wedge.
- 5: SERP intent is underserved and current leaders are materially weak, broken, or mismatched.

Do not infer this score from KD alone.

#### 3. Long-tail expansion — weight 12

Question: Can the opportunity expand into useful, non-duplicate pages and query classes?

- 0: One isolated query with no meaningful expansion.
- 1: Mostly spelling variants or mechanical keyword swaps.
- 2: A few adjacent pages, but limited independent value.
- 3: Several functional, scenario, guide, and comparison pages with distinct intent.
- 4: Broad but coherent matrix across features, scenarios, templates, guides, and languages.
- 5: Multiple scalable page systems with clear value and minimal doorway risk.

### B. Product differentiation — 32 points

#### 4. User and use-case difference — weight 12

Question: Is the candidate serving a meaningfully different user, task, moment, or success criterion from the host or competitors?

- 0: Same users, same task, same outcome.
- 1: Claimed audience difference without product consequences.
- 2: Minor scenario difference; most user needs remain the same.
- 3: Clear scenario or audience segment with different priorities.
- 4: Distinct user/job with materially different requirements and content needs.
- 5: Strongly distinct job-to-be-done, acquisition path, success metric, and retention reason.

#### 5. Homepage workflow difference — weight 10

Question: Can the first-screen interaction and default product path be materially different?

- 0: Same workflow; only copy or card ordering changes.
- 1: Cosmetic layout changes.
- 2: Minor default or optional control changes.
- 3: Different primary input/output or task sequence.
- 4: Clearly specialized workflow with different defaults, guidance, and result handling.
- 5: Distinct product experience that would be confusing if forced into the host homepage.

#### 6. Independent brand reason — weight 10

Question: Is there a concrete reason users would remember, choose, recommend, or return to the candidate as a separate product?

- 0: No reason beyond a new domain or name.
- 1: Generic promise shared by all competitors.
- 2: Narrow feature advantage that is easy to copy and weakly perceived.
- 3: Clear audience, outcome, trust, speed, quality, privacy, or workflow promise.
- 4: Defensible product position reinforced by experience, content, or assets.
- 5: Strong category association or unique product promise capable of earning branded demand.

### C. Independent growth — 19 points

#### 7. Link and distribution potential — weight 10

Question: Can the candidate earn independent links, mentions, shares, embeds, citations, or community distribution?

- 0: No credible linker, channel, or shareable asset.
- 1: Only generic directories or self-submitted links.
- 2: Some outreach targets, but weak reason to link.
- 3: Named audiences and assets with plausible link/share value.
- 4: Multiple repeatable channels or naturally shareable outputs/resources.
- 5: Strong linkable asset loop, embed/API/open-source/data advantage, or organic sharing behavior.

A score of 4–5 must identify who links, why, and to what asset.

#### 8. Independent content system — weight 9

Question: Can the site build a coherent content and page system that is not a duplicate of the host?

- 0: No content beyond the tool page.
- 1: Mostly rewritten host content.
- 2: A small guide set with heavy overlap.
- 3: Distinct guides, examples, templates, comparisons, or specifications.
- 4: Coherent topical system serving the candidate’s distinct audience and tasks.
- 5: Deep, extensible topical authority with proprietary examples, data, templates, or community assets.

### D. Site economics — 10 points

#### 9. Development and maintenance economics — weight 5

Question: Does the candidate fit the operator’s cost and maintenance constraints?

- 0: High service burden, unstable dependency, or prohibitive cost.
- 1: Heavy backend/API/customer-support burden.
- 2: Moderate recurring cost or frequent maintenance.
- 3: Manageable build and maintenance profile.
- 4: High code reuse, browser/local processing, low support burden.
- 5: Very low marginal cost, durable dependency profile, and clear operational leverage.

#### 10. Monetization fit — weight 5

Question: Is the traffic and product behavior compatible with the intended business model?

- 0: No credible monetization route or policy conflict.
- 1: Very weak commercial/advertising fit and poor user engagement.
- 2: Possible monetization but low-value geography, low page depth, or weak conversion.
- 3: Adequate fit with realistic assumptions.
- 4: Strong fit based on geography, intent, repeat use, page depth, or conversion potential.
- 5: Demonstrated monetization in comparable products or first-party evidence of strong unit economics.

## Separation risk score: 100 points

For risk criteria, 0 means no meaningful risk and 5 means severe risk.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Keyword overlap with host | 20 |
| Search-intent overlap | 20 |
| Product-workflow overlap | 15 |
| Content/template overlap | 15 |
| Brand-positioning ambiguity | 10 |
| Link-authority fragmentation | 10 |
| Development/maintenance fragmentation | 10 |

### Risk anchors

#### Keyword overlap with host
- 0: Distinct clusters with negligible collision.
- 3: Several shared terms but separable primary clusters.
- 5: Candidate and host target the same primary and secondary terms.

#### Search-intent overlap
- 0: Different jobs and outcomes.
- 3: Adjacent intent with some shared tasks.
- 5: Same intent and same desired result.

#### Product-workflow overlap
- 0: Materially different interaction and output.
- 3: Shared engine but specialized front-end path.
- 5: Same interface, defaults, and result.

#### Content/template overlap
- 0: Distinct content system.
- 3: Some reused components, unique core content.
- 5: Near-duplicate pages and template swaps.

#### Brand-positioning ambiguity
- 0: Clear, non-overlapping promises.
- 3: Positioning needs careful explanation.
- 5: Users cannot explain why both sites exist.

#### Link-authority fragmentation
- 0: Distinct linker audiences or assets.
- 3: Some competition for the same links.
- 5: The same outreach and links would be split across domains.

#### Development/maintenance fragmentation
- 0: Separate site creates negligible overhead.
- 3: Moderate duplicated operations.
- 5: Significant duplicated analytics, content, compliance, deployment, and support.

## Confidence is separate

Do not alter raw scores to compensate for missing data. Report a separate confidence level using `evidence-confidence.md`.
