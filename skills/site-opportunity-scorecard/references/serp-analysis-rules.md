# SERP Analysis Rules

The objective is not to summarize ten links. The objective is to identify whether a new product can enter the result set and why.

## Inspect these result attributes

For the primary cluster, record:

- ranking URL type: homepage, tool page, category, article, forum, documentation, marketplace, video;
- product specificity: direct solution, partial solution, informational substitute, irrelevant authority result;
- result freshness and maintenance;
- tool usability and completion path;
- page depth, content quality, localization, speed, mobile usability, privacy, and intrusive monetization;
- brand/authority advantage;
- backlink or citation barrier when reliable data is supplied;
- query-intent consistency across variations;
- special SERP features, AI summaries, videos, image packs, app results, or rich results;
- volatility or trend dependence.

## Weak SERP signals

A SERP is more breakable when several of these are true:

- ranking pages do not directly complete the user’s task;
- generic high-authority domains rank with thin or incidental relevance;
- tools are broken, slow, gated, outdated, or require unnecessary signup;
- result pages target adjacent rather than exact intent;
- multiple forum, documentation, or low-quality directory results occupy page one;
- local language or regional needs are poorly served;
- the current leaders have weak mobile workflows or intrusive ads;
- search results mix several intents, leaving a clear specialist wedge;
- dedicated new entrants already rank with modest authority.

## Strong SERP signals

A SERP is harder when:

- most results are highly relevant specialist products;
- leading tools have excellent workflows, strong brands, and extensive citations;
- the search engine strongly prefers a particular platform or official source;
- the query is navigational or controlled by a third-party brand;
- high-quality incumbents cover the same differentiated wedge;
- the product requires proprietary data or network effects unavailable to the candidate.

## Required conclusion

State:

1. dominant result type;
2. current intent interpretation;
3. weakest credible ranking competitors;
4. proposed entry wedge;
5. likely page type required to rank;
6. authority or link barrier;
7. confidence and missing evidence.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- Do not equate KD under 30 with a weak SERP.
- Do not claim a domain is weak from appearance alone.
- Do not treat CPC 0 as proof of no commercial value.
- Do not use search volume as the sole measure of opportunity.
- Do not count duplicated country/language pages as independent demand without localization evidence.
